About Gendered Toxicity

August 2, 2025

Today I received the final acceptance notification for my paper, "Gendered Toxicity in Competitive Gaming: Women's Perceptions and Responses."

I immediately felt excitement and relief. I thought about the journey it took to get here. I was originally motivated by the research gap in women's experiences in competitive gaming and my own experiences, having played League of Legends at various levels for more than a decade. This project about Gendered Toxicity is extremely special to me. My first PhD research project. My first time leading a research project. My first first author paper. And above all, being able to connect with the gaming community and work with women just like me. As a gamer-researcher and compassionate listener, I feel responsible to use my voice in a positive way. I am honored to be trusted with representing their experiences in academia.

Producing a piece like this in midst of rising and opposing ideologies was challenging. Numerous times, I was questioned by many others who thought I did not have the authority (who gives?) and dismissed my interests. I remember someone telling me, "Researching competitive League? You're not even challenger." "Women are all boosted anyway." I was not surprised. I knew that the gaming community was toxic. But this time, it inspired me even more to dig deeper into toxicity against women.

I was significantly touched by the stories of my participants, who are all women who play competitive games at all levels. Some women quit playing ranked and others became Game Changers winners. I believe that womens' experiences in gaming are endlessly unique, so it is not my purpose to be comprehensive. I only want to continue feminist conversations in gaming and push them further to inspire change and action.

In this research, I show that toxicity is extremely relevant towards women in many ways that academics and developers have yet to understand (or do, just have yet to acknowledge). This work teaches us about the toxicity women face, how they struggle, but most importantly, how they fight back and seek justice.

I'm excited to see the reaction to my work. I am continuously learning and improving my skills, my mindset, and my actions. I am just grateful to be officially part of the conversation and feel like my voice finally matters. I still feel like I have so much to say, so I hope to publish more when the ideas solidify and when the time is right.

Feel free to contact me if this work interests you or have any questions.
