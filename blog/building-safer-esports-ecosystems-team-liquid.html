Building Safer Esports Ecosystems: Reflections on Speaking with Team Liquid

November 30, 2025

This month, I had the incredible opportunity to join Team Liquid as a guest speaker. And I left feeling more inspired than ever! Esports organizations like TL operate at the vital intersection of players, fans, and game studios. Their position gives them a remarkable ability to influence healthier, safer gaming environments, and their genuine curiosity about the latest research in esports shows just how much potential there is for cross-industry collaboration.

During our session, we explored how research can meaningfully support competitive players and the organizations around them. A few key insights stood out:

1. Gendered Toxicity Remains a Barrier to Competitive Success

For women in esports, gendered toxicity is not just background noise. It is a profound obstacle that affects performance, opportunities, and long-term advancement. Recognizing this barrier is the first step toward removing it.

2. Women Are Innovators in Navigating Toxicity and Need to be Ready for the Main Stage

Women have developed countless adaptive strategies to thrive in hostile environments. Circuits like Game Changers offer vital competitive space for women and marginalized players, but the ultimate goal is integration on the main stages of esports. The desire is not separation but inclusion.

3. Ranked Titles Don’t Always Reflect True Skill

When players face active sabotage in competitive matches, traditional ranking systems fail to capture their real capabilities. We discussed the need for workshops with esports athletes themselves, which creates space to understand their challenges, goals, and the kinds of structural support required to help them succeed.

4. Early Exposure Matters

Many top-performing female esports athletes began gaming young, often in predominantly male spaces. Early exposure may help foster confidence and safety in mixed-gender competitive environments. Supporting young women in pursuing esports isn’t just beneficial—it’s necessary for long-term equity.

Opportunities like this remind me exactly why I do what I do. Research becomes powerful when it reaches the people who shape the future of play. Seeing Team Liquid’s engagement and commitment to understanding player needs was energizing, and I’m grateful for the chance to contribute to conversations that meaningfully influence the direction of competitive gaming.

While the session can't be shared publicly, these reflections capture the core of our discussion and the ongoing work ahead.
