How I navigated my internship

Reflections on my summer 2026 internship on Google’s People Analytics team in New York City.

← Back to News

I joined the People Analytics team expecting to work primarily with HR data and quantitative analysis. Instead, I had the opportunity to take on a project that was closer to user experience research: understanding how leaders use and navigate an internal HR tool.

The project brought together two needs. Accessibility had not been systematically assessed, and while the team knew some features were difficult to discover, they needed a clearer picture of users’ journeys and needs. Through conversations with stakeholders, I helped shape the research question and decide how to assess the experience. I developed a combined heuristic evaluation of usability and accessibility, then kept the work aligned with the product roadmap through regular check-ins with my host and team.

I conducted semi-structured interviews with 10 leaders, incorporating elements of cognitive walkthroughs to understand how people interpreted and navigated the tool. I approached the conversations as a curious listener, an approach I’ve developed through research with populations that can be understudied or difficult to reach. I also created personas to help the engineering and analytics teams better understand users’ contexts and use cases. Towards the end of my internship, I was able to connect my findings with a team actively developing the tool, helping bring user needs and accessibility considerations into their ongoing work. At the end, I presented my methodology and findings to stakeholders in multiple shareouts. It was really meaningful to hear the team showed interest and said they would review them as they considered next steps. While I didn’t have time to observe how the findings were implemented, I helped create a bridge between the research and the team’s product development efforts.

The evaluation surfaced accessibility considerations for a highly visual dashboard, including contrast and other issues affecting people with low vision. I used LLM tools to help organize documentation of potential issues, then reviewed the findings as part of my research process. Because much of the project is confidential, I can’t share detailed examples... but I can say that presenting the methodology and findings to stakeholders was an important part of the work.

I also learned to connect qualitative research with behavioral data: using SQL and Python to explore messy usage data, and thinking carefully about how to translate broad user goals into meaningful measures rather than treating clicks or engagement as the whole story. That balance of understanding what people do and why has made me especially interested in future UXR roles that combine research, product decisions, and data.

I’m grateful to my host, mentors, and team for giving me the autonomy to shape the project while offering thoughtful guidance along the way. I would have liked more time to see the findings through further iteration with the team, but the experience strengthened my interest in building products and internal tools around real user needs.
