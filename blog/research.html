Research

My work centers on how teens stay safe on Discord and in games, and how women experience and respond to toxicity in competitive gaming.

Gendered toxicity in games

This work looks at gendered toxicity in competitive gaming: the forms it takes, how women perceive and respond to it, and what that means for game design and moderation. I have also given a talk, Building Safer Esports Ecosystems, to Team Liquid (November 2025). See News for talks and recordings.

Gendered Toxicity in Competitive Gaming: Women's Perceptions and Responses
Elena Koung, Zinan Zhang, Xinning Gui, Yubo Kou
CHI PLAY 2025 · Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 9 (6), October 2025, 32 pages · DOI
Best Paper Honorable Mention

Women's participation in competitive gaming keeps growing, but research has paid little attention to their experiences of toxicity in competitive settings. I interviewed 28 women who play competitive games at different levels and analyzed the interviews with reflexive thematic analysis. The paper reports the forms of gendered toxicity women perceive and how they respond, including confrontational strategies aimed at justice and change. It argues that gendered toxicity upholds a sexist meritocracy and exposes the limits of existing interventions, and it encourages game developers and practitioners to build more inclusive moderation systems.

Discord and online safety

Discord mixes private DMs, semi-private voice channels, and public servers. This work asks how teens and children use it, what risks they meet, and how they, platforms, and designers can protect them.

Teen Vigilance: Navigating Risky Social Interactions on Discord
Elena Koung, Yunhan Liu, Zinan Zhang, Xinning Gui, Yubo Kou
CHI '26, April 13–17, 2026, Barcelona, Spain · ACM, 22 pages · DOI · arXiv

Based on 16 interviews with teenage Discord users, this paper looks at how teens handle risky social interactions. Teens practice vigilance: they size up suspicious interactions before becoming friends with someone, use safety tools, and take controlled risks to protect their privacy and security. At the community level, they join servers selectively, and vigilant governance structures support that. The paper argues that this shows teens' agency in managing risk and points to teen-centered designs for safer platforms.

Gaming Together on Discord: Teen Gamer's Cross-Platform Practices
Elena Koung, Xinning Gui, Yubo Kou
CHI PLAY 2026 · Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction 10 (7) · arXiv

This paper treats gaming together on Discord as a cross-platform practice that extends play beyond a single game and meets players' social needs. It also exposes players to social and security risks. The paper identifies a "platform gap," where governance is fragmented across platforms and leaves players exposed, and proposes design implications to bridge that gap, strengthen communication channels, and support safer gaming.
