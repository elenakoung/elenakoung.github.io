A Cognitive Walkthrough of Spotify Messages

October 15, 2025

In this project, we wanted to investigate Spotify's recently added messages feature. I was particularly interested in it since I had stumbled upon the feature on accident and wanted to evaluate its learnability. I was really curious if other users has the same experience, so working together with Sri and Yunhan, we collaborated on a Cognitive Walkthrough (CW), following Wharton’s 5-step CW process. In hindsight, we could've used the streamlined walkthrough method to get faster results. However, our study benefitted from using the full CW process because we could get more depth without sacrificing time, quality, and effort.

When Spotify introduced its new chat feature, we wanted to understand how easily users could learn to use it. Unlike playlists or recommendations, chat was a low-visibility feature—something new and unfamiliar in the Spotify experience.

To explore this, we ran a usability test focused on learnability. Our goal was to uncover what users assumed about how social features work in apps, and to identify where those assumptions might clash with the design team’s expectations.

Through this process, we aimed to reveal:

  • Gaps between how designers thought users would understand the feature and how users actually did.
  • How much users relied on their previous experience (in Spotify and with chat interfaces in general.)

We chose a Cognitive Walkthrough (CW) because it’s especially powerful for evaluating new or evolving features. CWs can be applied even in early design stages (like with paper prototypes), but in our case, we applied it to the released version to uncover remaining usability gaps.

What We Found: 4 Key Usability Challenges in Spotify Messages

Our evaluation surfaced several themes that explain why users struggled with the new Spotify chat feature.

Before users can even try the feature, they have to know it exists—and most didn’t. Some participants stumbled upon it by accident, while others assumed Spotify didn’t have a messaging option at all. Features that hide behind icons or menus can easily go unnoticed, especially when they’re new to a familiar app.

🧭 2. Learnability Challenges

Even after discovering chat, many users weren’t sure how to use it. The workflows relied heavily on prior knowledge from other chat apps or social platforms. If users didn’t already know what to expect, they struggled to understand how to start or continue a conversation.

🤔 3. Expectation Mismatches

In several cases, what users expected to happen didn’t match the actual result. For instance, sending a message or sharing a song didn’t always behave the way they thought it would. These gaps between mental models and design intent created moments of confusion and friction.

📣 4. Lack of Feedback

Finally, users wanted clearer feedback about what was happening. Was their message sent? Did the song share work? Without clear status indicators or confirmations, people were left guessing whether their actions succeeded.

💡 Recommendations for Improving Spotify Messages

Based on what we learned from the usability study, here are a few ways Spotify could make the chat experience more intuitive, engaging, and fun for users:

🔍 1. Make Messages Easier to Find

Right now, Spotify Chat is somewhat hidden. Increasing its visibility, for example by placing it in a more prominent spot in the navigation or highlighting it with a subtle prompt, would help users discover it naturally instead of by accident.

🎵 2. Enable Sharing Songs and Playlists Directly in Messages

Music is at the heart of Spotify, so why limit chat to text? Allowing users to send songs, playlists, or even albums directly in messages would make sharing music seamless and social.

⚡ 3. Add Quick-Action Buttons Beside Shared Music

When a song or playlist appears in a chat, adding action buttons such as play, save, or add to playlist would make interactions faster and smoother. Users could take action without leaving the conversation.

🤝 4. Emphasize Collaborative Playlists and Icon Consistency

Collaborative playlists are one of Spotify’s most social features. Making them easier to create and recognize, with clear and consistent icons, would strengthen the sense of connection between users.

😄 5. Expand Quick Reply Options

Finally, make chatting more expressive. Adding a wider range of quick replies, like emojis or reaction icons, would help users respond more naturally and keep conversations lively.

✨ Conclusion and Next Steps

Spotify Messages shows real promise in transforming how users connect through music. While our study highlighted key challenges in discoverability and workflow efficiency, it also revealed strong potential for deeper social engagement on the platform.

Moving forward, I'm excited to continue exploring how users share, collaborate, and express themselves through Spotify. I'd love to hear feedback and have conversations with others working on social design and music interaction. Together, we can shape a more connected, creative listening experience. 🎧
