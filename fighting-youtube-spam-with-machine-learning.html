Fighting YouTube Spam with Machine Learning

December 11, 2025

In the age of social media, user-generated content is a goldmine for understanding audience engagement. However, that gold is often buried under a mountain of spam: irrelevant, repetitive, or malicious content that clutters comment sections and spreads misinformation. For this project, I developed a machine learning pipeline to automatically classify YouTube music video comments as "spam" or "not spam."

The Dataset

Using a dataset from Kaggle, I analyzed 1,884 comments (cleaned to 1,592 rows) collected over two years from popular music videos like "Gangnam Style". The goal was simple: determine which classification model best distinguishes genuine interaction from unwanted noise.

The Data Pipeline

Turning raw, messy text into a format suitable for machine learning required a rigorous preprocessing workflow:

  • Text Refinement: I applied lowercasing, tokenization, stemming, and a stop-word filter to reduce noise and standardize the vocabulary.
  • Feature Engineering: I used TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) to weight terms by their relative importance, highlighting discriminative words for the classification task.
  • Addressing Imbalance: Because the labels were unevenly distributed, I applied SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique). This improved the Naïve Bayes model’s accuracy by approximately 2% by better balancing the classes.

Model Benchmarking

I evaluated four distinct models to find the optimal balance of speed and predictive power:

  1. Gradient Boosted Trees (Champion): This model was the top performer, achieving an F1-score of 95.7%. It excelled at capturing the subtle, non-linear interactions within the TF-IDF features.
  2. Naïve Bayes (Speed): While it struggled with sensitivity (81.6%), it was the fastest model to train, finishing in just 5 seconds.
  3. Random Forest: Robust to noise and high-dimensional data, it reached a 93.6% F1-score but required a longer training time of approximately two minutes.
  4. Logistic Regression: Served as a strong baseline, utilizing L1 and L2 regularization to prevent overfitting on the thousands of generated TF-IDF features.

Key Takeaways & Future Directions

The results demonstrate that even with relatively simple TF-IDF features, ensemble tree-based models can achieve high accuracy in spam detection. However, this approach has limitations: TF-IDF is not context-sensitive, meaning it can miss nuanced language.

Future iterations of this project will focus on:

  • Implementing Deep Learning architectures like LSTM, CNN, or transformer-based models (BERT) to capture context.
  • Expanding the dataset to include diverse genres to improve generalizability.
  • Conducting temporal analysis to track how spamming trends evolve over time.

By automating this filtering process, platforms and creators can significantly improve the viewer experience, ensuring the focus remains on genuine community engagement.
