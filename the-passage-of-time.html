The Passage of Time: Self-doubt, Qualifying exam & First paper acceptance

October 6, 2025

oh... hello

I stayed away from blogging because website builders are hard to navigate and honestly I preferred spending my time learning new meta builds on League rather than dedicate time to pretty-fy and make my personal website functional.

This is a reflection of my 1st year as a PhD student and is hopefully something I could look back at later to be entertained by.

I quit my job, moved out of Chicago, and solo travelled to Malta, Germany, and Taiwan. Looking back, I took a huge risk to follow my interests because I was giving up a more than comfortable lifestyle and ideal living situation just because I was "bored." My career journey so far has just been "following what's interesting to me" and for a long time that has been playing League. If I can't play League for a living, then I might as well have a job that allows me to play on the clock. But even that became unsatisfactory because the amount of time spent on unfulfilling work was eating away at my soul.

I hoped to find more fulfillment in research. But it was also hard to explain what I was researching and what my PhD is even about. It became easier to joke about my PhD being "studying games" as that's memorable.

things I learned:

  1. do NOT call a taxi in Germany.
  2. succulents can get sunburnt.
  3. it's okay to miss the life you had.

fall semester

I was really excited to get started on research, meet my cohort, and absorb information. I welcomed the challenges that came along with this transition. I started my first project, from IRB approval to conducting every interview. I was learning by doing, but did I really know what I was doing? Classwork was easy so I spent a good amount of time playing League at first, but this creeped into my research time. Thus I had to cut down my time gaming by a lot, something I'd never done in the last 6 years. It hurt my rank (and my ego) because I ended up being hardstuck diamond but I'm still coping and saying that it's only because I don't play enough anymore.

things I learned:

  1. i'm a masters peaker
  2. nothing lasts forever
  3. apple pie cookies

winter break

things I learned:

  1. enjoy interviewing participants
  2. nyc food is unbeatable
  3. my wallet is not very deep

spring semester (winter semester)

After submitting my first paper, I realized I really wanted to manage my time better. But I didn't really know what was the best way so I tried to just plan out my weeks in advance. Of course, nothing goes to plan. I even tried to plan my Qualifying Exam (QE) but it also didn't go to plan. I had a conditional pass that meant I needed to heavily revise it. So far, every time I need to make revisions it makes me feel like I don't even know how to write. Like I'm avoiding reading my own writing because it is sooo bad. Through this process, I learned that it's okay to not have a perfect idea or section, but have strong logic. Once I had a logical story, it became much easier to write.

things I learned:

  1. 13 out of 15 weeks are freezing and half of it is rainy
  2. cherry blossoms leave too quickly
  3. eat less sugar

summer "vacation"

I really felt that these last few months were a true summer. I got to go back home to Texas, have a lot of personal time, and enjoy life outside of research. I visited Washington D.C. and Boston. Super lovely to catch up with friends and enjoy yummy food. But towards the ends, we approached the CHI 2026 deadline and I needed to grind it. I learned so much about writing then. I really wanted to rush the process because I was so scared I didn't have enough time, but the reality is that you make time. I had to challenge all of my existing mindsets about friends, relationships, writing process, and so much more. But by doing so, I was able to grow and improve on all of these things in my life.

things I learned:

  1. trust in the process
  2. be kind
  3. zucchini is an elite summer produce

So I passed my qualifying exam, got my first ever paper accepted, and still face imposter syndrome. But I'm proud of my growth in the last year. Especially in my Yone gameplay!
